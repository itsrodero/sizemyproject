How to use the insulation calculator

  1. Measure the attic floor — usually the footprint of the heated part of the house below it.
  2. Choose your climate zone. The labels are a rough guide; the U.S. Department of Energy publishes a county-level climate zone map.
  3. Tell it what's there now. Measure the depth of the existing insulation. If it's about 3–4 inches, choose that option; otherwise enter its R-value (depth × R per inch for its type).
  4. Pick the insulation you'll add and, for bags, enter the coverage from the bag's chart at your target R-value.

  3. With cellulose at R-3.5 per inch: 14 inches

Before you add insulation

  • Seal air leaks first — around chimneys, plumbing and wiring holes, recessed lights and the attic hatch. Air leaks can undo much of the benefit.
  • Keep ventilation open: install baffles at the eaves so insulation doesn't block soffit vents.
  • Mind clearances around chimneys, flues and recessed lights that aren't rated for insulation contact.
  • Deal with moisture — wet or moldy insulation points to a roof leak or ventilation problem that needs fixing first.

Frequently asked questions

How many inches of insulation is R-49?

Roughly 18 to 22 inches of blown-in fiberglass, 13 to 15 inches of cellulose, or 13 to 17 inches of fiberglass batts.

Can I put new insulation on top of old?

Usually yes, if the old insulation is dry and free of mold or pests. Don't put faced batts (with a vapor retarder) on top of existing insulation — use unfaced batts or loose fill.

How many bags of blown-in insulation do I need?

Divide your attic area by the coverage per bag listed on the bag's chart for your target R-value. Coverage per bag drops as the target R-value rises.
