What is a board foot?

A board foot is a volume of wood equal to a board 1 inch thick, 12 inches wide and 1 foot long — 144 cubic inches. Lumberyards and hardwood dealers price wood by the board foot because it lets them compare boards of any size: a 2-inch-thick board 6 inches wide holds exactly as much wood per foot as a 1-inch board 12 inches wide.

How to calculate board feet

Board feet = thickness (in) × width (in) × length (ft) ÷ 12 = thickness (in) × width (in) × length (in) ÷ 144 Cost = board feet × price per board foot

Worked example: ten 2×6 boards, 8 ft long

  1. One board: 2 × 6 × 8 ÷ 12 = 8 board feet
  2. Ten boards: 80 board feet

Worked example: one 8/4 walnut board, 7 in wide and 6 ft long, at $12 per board foot

  1. 8/4 means 8 quarters of an inch, so 2 inches thick
  2. 2 × 7 × 6 ÷ 12 = 7 board feet, costing 7 × $12 = $84

Nominal or actual size?

Construction lumber is sold by its nominal size: a 2×4 actually measures about 1½ × 3½ inches after drying and planing. Dimensional softwood lumber is usually priced using the nominal size, so a 2×4 counts as 2 × 4 inches. Hardwood dealers work differently: they measure rough-sawn boards by thickness in quarters of an inch and by their actual width and length.

Frequently asked questions

How many board feet are in a 2×4×8?

2 × 4 × 8 ÷ 12 = 5⅓ board feet, using the nominal size.

Is a board foot the same as a linear foot?

No. A linear foot is just length, whatever the size of the board. A board foot is a volume, so a foot of 2×12 is 2 board feet while a foot of 1×4 is a third of a board foot.

How many board feet in a cubic foot?

12. A cubic foot is 1,728 cubic inches, and a board foot is 144 cubic inches.

What is a board foot in metric?

One board foot is about 2.36 liters, or 0.00236 cubic meters. A cubic meter is about 424 board feet.
