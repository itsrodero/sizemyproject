The layers of a paver patio

Layer (top to bottom)Typical thicknessMaterial
Pavers2⅜ in (60 mm) is standard for patiosConcrete pavers, brick or stone
Bedding sandAbout 1 in, screeded flatCoarse, washed concrete sand
Base4–6 in compacted (more on clay or in cold climates)Crushed stone or road base with fines, or clean open-graded stone
Subgrade—Native soil, stripped of topsoil and compacted

The Interlocking Concrete Pavement Institute's guidance for pedestrian areas like patios and walkways is a compacted aggregate base of about 4 to 6 inches under roughly an inch of bedding sand; driveways need substantially more base.

How deep to dig

Excavation depth = paver thickness + sand (1 in) + base thickness

Example: standard 2⅜ in pavers on a 4 in base

  1. 2⅜ + 1 + 4 = about 7⅜ in below the finished patio surface
  2. With a 6 in base: about 9⅜ in

Measure from where you want the finished surface to be — typically flush with the lawn at the edges and at least an inch or so below any door threshold.

Extend the base past the edges

The edge restraint needs solid ground to be spiked into, and the edge pavers carry loads from feet and furniture right at the border. Dig and build the base 6 to 12 inches wider than the patio on every side that isn't against a wall. Include that extra width when you calculate the base with the gravel calculator — or let the 10% allowance in the paver calculator cover it on small patios.

Slope for drainage

A patio must shed water away from the house. A common target is a fall of about ⅛ to ¼ inch per foot (1–2%). Build the slope into the subgrade and base — not the sand — so every layer is an even thickness.

Example: a patio extending 12 ft from the house

  1. At ¼ in per foot: 12 × ¼ = 3 in lower at the far edge
  2. At ⅛ in per foot: 1½ in lower

Compacting the base

  • Compact in lifts of about 2 to 4 inches with a plate compactor; a single thick layer only compacts on top.
  • Moisten dense-graded base slightly so the fines bind; it compacts poorly when bone dry or soaking wet.
  • Check flatness with a long straightedge and string lines before spreading sand. Fix low spots with base material, not extra sand.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use sand instead of gravel as the base?

No — sand alone shifts and washes out. Sand is only the thin bedding layer; the load-bearing base is compacted crushed stone.

Do I need landscape fabric under the base?

On clay or wet soils, a geotextile between the soil and the base keeps them from mixing and is often recommended. Weeds in the joints come from seeds blown in on top, which polymeric joint sand helps prevent.

How long does the base take?

Longer than laying the pavers. Excavation and compaction are most of the work — budget your time accordingly and consider renting a small excavator for large patios.
