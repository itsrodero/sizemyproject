The short answer

Plan on two coats. Even good paints rarely give perfectly even color and sheen in one coat over a previously painted wall. A second coat evens out roller marks, touch-up spots and thin areas, and adds durability.

When one coat can be enough

  • Repainting the same or a very similar color over a clean, sound, previously painted surface.
  • Using a high-hide paint labeled for one-coat coverage — though a second coat is still often needed for an even finish.
  • Refreshing a ceiling in the same white.

When you need primer

SurfaceWhy prime
New drywall and joint compoundBare paper and compound absorb paint differently; primer seals them so the top coat looks even
Bare woodSeals the grain; stain-blocking primer stops tannins bleeding through
Water stains, smoke, markerStain-blocking primer keeps them from showing through
Dark to light (or light to deep color)A primer — tinted toward the new color for deep colors — cuts the number of top coats
Glossy paintHelps adhesion after cleaning and scuffing the surface
Patches and repairsSpot-priming prevents dull "flashing" spots in the finish

When you may need three coats

  • Deep reds, yellows and other saturated colors, which often have less hiding power.
  • Covering a very dark color with a light one without primer.
  • Rough or highly textured surfaces.

Paint-and-primer products

"Paint and primer in one" products build a thicker film and hide well over previously painted surfaces. For bare drywall, stains or glossy surfaces, a dedicated primer is still the more reliable — and usually cheaper — first coat.

How coats change the quantity

Gallons = wall area × number of coats ÷ coverage per gallon

With a typical coverage of about 350 sq ft per gallon, a 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls (about 333 sq ft after a door and two windows) needs about 1 gallon per coat — 2 gallons for two coats. Primer usually covers less area per gallon than paint; check the can. The paint calculator handles coats, primer and ceilings.

Recoat times

Wait for each coat to dry to the recoat time on the can before applying the next — typically a few hours for latex paints, longer in cool or humid conditions. Painting over a coat that's still soft can drag and leave marks.

Frequently asked questions

Is one coat of paint enough on new drywall?

No. Prime first, then apply two coats of paint for an even finish.

Do I need to prime before repainting the same color?

Usually not, as long as the surface is clean and sound. Spot-prime any patches.

Can I use primer instead of a second coat?

No — primer isn't designed as a finish and doesn't have the durability or sheen of paint.
