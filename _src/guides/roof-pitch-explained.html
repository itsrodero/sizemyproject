What roof pitch means

Roof pitch (or slope) is written as rise over run: how many inches the roof rises for every 12 inches it runs horizontally. A 6/12 roof rises 6 inches for every foot. The higher the first number, the steeper the roof.

PitchAngleDescription
2/12–3/129.5°–14°Low slope; shingles need special underlayment
4/12–6/1218.4°–26.6°Common, walkable with care
7/12–9/1230.3°–36.9°Steep; roof jacks or a harness needed
12/1245°Very steep, typical of A-frames and some Victorian roofs

How to measure pitch

  1. From the attic (safest): hold a level horizontally against the underside of a rafter, mark 12 inches along the level, and measure straight up from that mark to the rafter. That measurement is the rise.
  2. From a ladder at the gable: do the same against the rake board at the edge of the roof.
  3. With your phone: many inclinometer and pitch-gauge apps read the angle when you lay the phone on a rafter or photograph the gable end.
Angle (degrees) = arctan(rise ÷ 12) Pitch factor = √(1 + (rise ÷ 12)²)

Why pitch changes the amount of material

A sloped roof has more surface than the footprint it covers. The pitch factor converts one into the other: multiply the footprint (including overhangs) by the factor to get the real roof area. A 6/12 roof has 11.8% more surface than its footprint; a 12/12 roof has 41% more.

Example: a 30 × 40 ft footprint

  1. At 4/12: 1,200 × 1.054 = 1,265 sq ft
  2. At 8/12: 1,200 × 1.202 = 1,442 sq ft — about 1.8 squares (5 or 6 bundles) more than the 4/12 roof

The roofing calculator applies the factor automatically and turns the area into squares and bundles.

Pitch limits for roofing materials

  • Asphalt shingles aren't used below 2/12. Between 2/12 and 4/12, manufacturers and the building code require extra measures such as double underlayment.
  • Low-slope roofs below 2/12 need membrane roofing (for example EPDM, TPO or modified bitumen).
  • Metal roofing has its own minimum slopes depending on the panel and seam type.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common roof pitch?

Many US homes have roofs between 4/12 and 9/12, with 6/12 being very common.

Is a 4/12 roof walkable?

Generally yes with good footwear and dry conditions. Anything steeper than about 7/12 needs roof jacks or fall protection.

How do I convert pitch to degrees?

Take the arctangent of rise ÷ 12. For 6/12: arctan(0.5) = 26.6°.
