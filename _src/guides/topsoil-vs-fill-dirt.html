Why fill dirt belongs underneath

Fill dirt is chosen because it compacts well and stays put: it has little organic matter to rot and settle over time. That's exactly what you want under a patio, a shed or when raising the grade of a yard — and exactly what you don't want for plants to grow in. Put fill in the lower layers, compact it, and finish with topsoil where things will grow.

How deep to use each

  • New lawn: 4 to 6 inches of topsoil over loosened subsoil.
  • Top-dressing a lawn: about ¼ to ½ inch of topsoil or compost, raked in.
  • Garden beds: 6 to 12 inches of loose, fertile soil; mix compost into the top layer.
  • Raising grade: fill in compacted layers of a few inches at a time, then topsoil on top.

Buying tips

  • See it before it's dumped. Good screened topsoil is dark, crumbly and free of rocks, roots, construction debris and weed-filled clods.
  • Ask about the source and screening. "Topsoil" isn't a regulated term; quality varies widely between suppliers.
  • Buy by the yard when you can. Soil sold by the ton includes the weight of its water — wet soil can weigh a lot more for the same volume.
  • Plan the drop spot. A few cubic yards is a big pile; tarp the driveway and have it dumped close to where it's needed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use fill dirt for a lawn?

Not on its own. Grass struggles in fill because it lacks organic matter and nutrients. Use fill to build up the grade and finish with at least 4 inches of topsoil.

Is topsoil the same as garden soil?

No. Garden soil is topsoil enriched with compost or other organic matter for planting beds; it's usually too rich and loose for filling or leveling.

Does soil settle after delivery?

Yes, especially once it's raked and watered. Order about 10% extra, and fill low spots in layers.
