What deck mud is

Deck mud — also called dry-pack mortar or floor mud — is a stiff mix of sand and Portland cement with just enough water to hold together when you squeeze it. Because it hardly slumps, you can pack and screed it into a sloped shower pan or a flat mortar bed that tile is set on. You can buy it premixed in bags or mix your own from sand and cement.

How to use the deck mud calculator

  1. Measure the shower floor inside the curb and walls.
  2. Measure from the drain to the farthest wall. With a drain in the middle that's about half the length; with a linear drain along one wall, it's the full width of the floor.
  3. Set the thickness at the drain and the slope. The bed gets thicker as it rises toward the walls — usually ¼ inch for every foot.
  4. Choose premixed bags or your own mix to see what to buy.

How the volume is calculated

Rise to the wall = slope (in/ft) × drain-to-wall distance (ft) Thickness at wall = thickness at drain + rise Average thickness ≈ thickness at drain + rise ÷ 2 Volume (ft³) = floor area (ft²) × average thickness (in) ÷ 12 80 lb bags = volume ÷ 0.66 ft³ per bag (round up)

Worked example: a 5 × 3 ft shower with a center drain, 1 inch thick at the drain

  1. Drain to farthest wall: 2.5 ft, so the rise is 2.5 × ¼ = ⅝ in and the bed is 1⅝ in thick at the wall
  2. Average thickness: 1 + ⅝ ÷ 2 ≈ 1.31 in
  3. Volume: 15 sq ft × 1.31 ÷ 12 = 1.64 ft³, plus 10% = 1.8 cubic feet
  4. That's 3 bags of 80 lb premixed floor mud (1.8 ÷ 0.66 = 2.7)

Mixing your own deck mud

A common home-made mix is 4 to 5 parts sand to 1 part Portland cement by volume, with just enough water that a handful holds its shape when squeezed but crumbles when you tap it. Because the cement fills the gaps between the sand grains, plan on roughly as much sand as the finished volume of mud, plus the cement on top of that.

Shower pan tips

  • Mind the slope. Too flat and water ponds; much steeper than ¼ inch per foot and the floor feels uneven underfoot.
  • Check the drain height first. It sets the thickness at the drain, and the wall thickness follows from the slope. A drain set too high is the usual reason for an unexpectedly thick, heavy bed.
  • Follow your waterproofing system. Traditional pans use a sloped pre-bed under a liner and a second mortar bed on top; surface-applied membranes go over a single sloped bed. Each system specifies its own minimum thicknesses.
  • Keep it dry-ish. Too much water makes the mud shrink and crack as it cures.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of deck mud for a shower pan?

A typical 5 × 3 ft shower, 1 inch thick at the drain with a ¼ inch per foot slope, needs about 1.8 cubic feet — 3 bags of 80 lb premixed floor mud.

How much does an 80 lb bag of floor mud cover?

QUIKRETE's data sheet gives about 0.66 cubic feet per 80 lb bag — roughly 8 square feet at 1 inch thick, or 5 square feet at 1½ inches.

What is the ratio for deck mud?

Most installers use 4 or 5 parts sand to 1 part Portland cement by volume, mixed stiff.

How thick should a shower mortar bed be?

Usually at least ¾ inch at the drain, getting thicker toward the walls with the slope. Your drain and waterproofing system's instructions set the exact minimum.
