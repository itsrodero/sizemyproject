How to use the mulch calculator

  1. Measure each bed. Use Rectangle for straight beds and borders, Circle for rings around trees, and split curved beds into simple shapes you can add up.
  2. Choose the depth. For a new bed, use 3 inches. If there's still mulch from last year, measure what's left and only add the difference.
  3. Read the result in cubic yards (how bulk mulch is sold) and in 2 and 3 cubic foot bags (how garden centers sell it).

How to calculate mulch

Volume (ft³) = area (ft²) × depth (in) ÷ 12 Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27 2 cu ft bags = cubic feet ÷ 2 (round up) 3 cu ft bags = cubic feet ÷ 3 (round up)

Worked example: a 4 × 25 ft border, 3 inches deep

  1. Area: 4 × 25 = 100 sq ft
  2. Volume: 100 × 3 ÷ 12 = 25 ft³ = 0.93 yd³
  3. Bags: 25 ÷ 2 = 12.5, so 13 bags of 2 cu ft (14 with 5% extra), or 9 bags of 3 cu ft.

Worked example: a ring 6 ft across around a young tree, 3 inches deep

  1. Area: π × 3² = 28.3 sq ft
  2. Volume: 28.3 × 3 ÷ 12 = 7.1 ft³, so 4 bags of 2 cu ft. Leave a mulch-free gap of at least 4 inches around the trunk.

How deep should mulch be?

University of Illinois Extension recommends a layer of 2 to 4 inches — 2 to 3 inches of composted or fine mulch, and 3 to 4 inches of coarse mulches such as wood chips and bark. More isn't better: thick layers can mat down, reduce the water and oxygen that reach the roots, and cause decay.

  • Keep it off trunks and stems. Pull mulch at least 4 inches away from the trunk. "Volcano" mulching piled against the bark is one of the most common causes of tree decline.
  • Top up, don't pile on. Check the depth before adding more. If there's still 2 inches, rake it to refresh the look and add only an inch.
  • Go wide around trees. Spread mulch out toward the drip line rather than deep against the trunk.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of mulch are in a yard?

13.5 bags of 2 cubic feet, or 9 bags of 3 cubic feet. A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet.

How much mulch do I need for 100 square feet?

At 3 inches deep, 100 sq ft needs 25 cubic feet — about 0.93 cubic yards or 13 bags of 2 cu ft.

Should I remove old mulch before adding new?

Not usually. Measure the depth first: if the old layer is still 2 inches or more, just loosen it and add a thin top dressing. Remove it if it's matted, moldy or piled against trunks.

Does mulch settle?

Yes — fresh, fluffy mulch settles after the first rains, and organic mulch slowly breaks down. That's why the calculator adds a little extra by default.
