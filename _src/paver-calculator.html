How to use the paver calculator

  1. Measure the finished paved area. Split L-shapes into rectangles and run the calculator for each part, or type the total square footage.
  2. Choose the paver size printed on the product. Most concrete pavers are sold by nominal size; if yours list exact dimensions, pick Custom size.
  3. Set the extra for cuts. 10% covers edge cuts on a simple layout. Herringbone patterns, curves and lots of corners waste more — use 15%.
  4. Check the base depths and read how much gravel, sand and edging to order along with the pavers.

How to calculate pavers

Pavers per sq ft = 144 ÷ ((paver length + joint) × (paver width + joint)) [inches] Pavers = area (sq ft) × pavers per sq ft × (1 + extra %) Base (yd³) = area × base depth (ft) × 1.10 ÷ 27 Sand (yd³) = area × sand depth (ft) × 1.10 ÷ 27

Worked example: a 12 × 12 ft patio with 6 × 9 in pavers

  1. Area: 144 sq ft. Pavers per sq ft: 144 ÷ (6 × 9) = 2.67
  2. Pavers: 144 × 2.67 = 384, plus 10% = 423 pavers
  3. Base, 4 in: 144 × 0.333 × 1.10 ÷ 27 = 1.96 yd³ — about 2.9 tons of road base
  4. Sand, 1 in: 144 × 0.083 × 1.10 = 13.2 ft³ — 0.49 yd³ or 27 bags of 50 lb

What goes under pavers

A paver patio is only as good as what's underneath it. The Interlocking Concrete Pavement Institute's guidance for a typical installation is a compacted aggregate base with a thin, even layer of bedding sand on top:

LayerPatios and walkwaysDriveways
Compacted gravel base (crushed stone or road base)4–6 in8–12 in
Bedding sand (coarse, washed)About 1 in, screeded — not compacted before layingAbout 1 in
Joint sandSwept into the joints after laying; polymeric sand resists weeds and washout
  • Go deeper on clay, poorly drained soil or in cold climates where frost heave is a risk.
  • Compact the base in lifts of 2 to 4 inches with a plate compactor rather than all at once.
  • Extend the base 6 inches or so beyond the pavers so the edge restraint sits on solid ground. The calculator's 10% base allowance helps cover this.
  • Don't use extra sand to fix a lumpy base. Level the gravel instead; thick sand shifts and ruts.

Edge restraint and joint sand

Pavers need an edge restraint — plastic or aluminum edging spiked into the base, a concrete curb or a soldier course set in mortar — so they can't creep outward. Buy edging to match the perimeter the calculator shows, minus any side that butts against a house wall or existing slab.

Joint sand coverage depends heavily on paver size and joint width, so check the coverage printed on the bag. Small pavers have far more joints per square foot than large slabs and use much more sand.

Frequently asked questions

How many pavers do I need for a 10 × 10 patio?

100 square feet with 6 × 9 in pavers takes 267 pavers, or 294 with 10% extra. With 4 × 8 in pavers it's 450, or 495 with 10% extra.

How much gravel do I need under pavers?

For a 4-inch compacted base, about 1.4 cubic yards per 100 square feet including 10% for compaction — roughly 2 tons of road base.

How much extra should I buy?

10% for a simple rectangle in a running bond or stack pattern; 15% for herringbone, diagonal layouts or curved edges. Keep a few spares from the same batch for future repairs, since colors vary between production runs.

Can I lay pavers directly on sand?

Not for a lasting patio. Sand alone shifts under load and in wet weather. Use a compacted gravel base with a thin layer of bedding sand on top.
