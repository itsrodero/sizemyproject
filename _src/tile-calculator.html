How to use the tile calculator

  1. Measure the area to tile. For a floor, measure wall to wall. For a wall or backsplash, measure the tiled height and width and subtract windows or cabinets.
  2. Choose the tile size and grout joint. Joints slightly reduce the number of tiles, which matters most for small tiles.
  3. Add extra for cuts: 10% for straight layouts, 15% for diagonal layouts, large-format tile or rooms with many corners.
  4. Enter the coverage per box to get the number of boxes.

How to calculate tile

One tile covers (sq ft) = (tile length + joint) × (tile width + joint) ÷ 144 Tiles = area ÷ one tile's coverage × (1 + extra %) Boxes = area × (1 + extra %) ÷ coverage per box

Worked example: a 10 × 8 ft bathroom floor in 12 × 12 in tile, ⅛ in joints

  1. Area: 80 sq ft
  2. One tile with joint: 12.125 × 12.125 ÷ 144 = 1.021 sq ft
  3. Tiles: 80 ÷ 1.021 = 78.4, plus 10% → 87 tiles

Choosing a grout joint

  • Rectified tiles (precisely cut edges) allow narrow joints, often 1/16 to 1/8 in.
  • Pressed or handmade tiles vary in size and need wider joints, often 3/16 in or more.
  • Large-format tile needs a joint wide enough to absorb small size differences; follow the manufacturer's recommendation.

Other materials to plan for

  • Thinset mortar: coverage per bag depends on the trowel notch, which depends on tile size — larger tiles need a bigger notch and more mortar. Use the coverage chart on the bag.
  • Grout: depends on tile size, thickness and joint width; small tiles with wide joints use far more. Grout manufacturers publish calculators for their products.
  • Backer board or membrane for wet areas, and edge trim where tile meets other flooring or ends on a wall.

Frequently asked questions

How many 12 × 12 tiles do I need for 100 square feet?

About 98 with ⅛ in joints, or 108 with 10% extra for cuts.

How much extra tile should I buy?

10% for straight layouts; 15% for diagonal patterns, herringbone, large-format tile or small rooms with many cuts. Keep spares from the same lot for repairs.

Should I subtract the area under the vanity or tub?

Yes for a fixed tub or built-in cabinet. For a freestanding vanity or tub, tile underneath so it can be moved or replaced.
