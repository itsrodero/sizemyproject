How long does concrete take to cure?

New concrete hardens in hours but keeps gaining strength for weeks. Here's when it's safe to walk on, drive on and seal, and how to cure it so it reaches full strength.

The short answer

For normal concrete at around 70°F (21°C), these are the usual milestones. Cold weather stretches every one of them, and the instructions on a bag or from your ready-mix supplier always take priority.

Time after pouringWhat you can usually do
A few hoursThe surface has set: finishing is done, but it marks easily
24 to 48 hoursWalk on it carefully; strip forms from slabs and walkways
About 7 daysDrive a car on a driveway; roughly two-thirds of design strength
28 daysDesign strength — heavy trucks, RVs and most sealers, paints and stains

"Cure" in the strict sense means keeping concrete moist and warm while the cement reacts with water. That reaction is fastest in the first days and continues slowly for months, which is why 28 days is used as the standard point at which concrete's strength is measured.

Curing is not drying

Concrete doesn't harden by drying out. It hardens because the cement reacts chemically with water — a process called hydration. If the surface dries too early, the reaction stops near the top, leaving a weaker, dusty surface that cracks and wears more easily. That's why the first week matters so much: the goal is to keep water in, not to get it out.

How to cure concrete

Industry guidance, such as the American Concrete Institute's, calls for keeping new concrete moist for at least the first seven days. Any of these methods works for a home project:

  • Keep it wet. Mist or sprinkle the surface several times a day once it has set hard enough not to mark, so it never dries out completely.
  • Cover it. Plastic sheeting or wet burlap laid over the slab holds moisture in. Weigh down the edges, and keep the cover flat — wrinkles can leave light and dark patches on the surface.
  • Use a curing compound. A spray-on membrane, applied after finishing, seals moisture in with one coat. Check that it's compatible with any sealer, paint or flooring you plan to put on later.

How weather changes curing time

  • Cold weather: below about 50°F (10°C), hydration slows down a lot, and every milestone takes longer. Concrete must not freeze while it's young — the usual rule is to protect it until it reaches 500 psi, which typically takes the first day or two. Insulated curing blankets help, and you should avoid pouring on frozen ground.
  • Hot, dry or windy weather: the surface can lose water faster than it bleeds up, causing plastic shrinkage cracks within hours. Pour early in the day, dampen the base and forms beforehand, and start curing as soon as the surface can take it.

When can you…

  • Walk on it? Usually after 24 to 48 hours. Avoid dragging things, turning on the spot or letting pets run on it in the first few days.
  • Remove the forms? Forms for slabs, paths and patios usually come off after one to two days. Formwork that holds weight, such as for steps or walls, needs longer.
  • Drive on it? Passenger cars after about seven days. Wait the full 28 days for heavy vehicles, trailers and RVs, and keep them away from the edges, which are the weakest part of a slab.
  • Set fence posts or a mailbox? Standard concrete around a post needs a day or two before you hang heavy gates or boards. Fast-setting post mixes are made to carry loads within hours — follow the time on the bag.
  • Seal, paint or stain it? Many sealers and coatings ask for 28 days so that enough moisture has left the slab. Read the product label; some "cure and seal" products are made to go on fresh concrete.
  • Lay flooring on a new slab? Drying for flooring is a separate question from curing. A thick slab can take months to dry enough for wood, vinyl or tile adhesive, so flooring makers specify a moisture test rather than a number of days.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a 4-inch slab take to cure?

The usual milestones apply: walk on it after a day or two, drive a car on it after about a week, and treat it as fully cured at 28 days. Thickness matters less for curing than for drying — a thicker slab takes longer to dry out for flooring.

Does concrete cure faster in hot weather?

It gains strength faster, but hot, dry or windy conditions also dry the surface faster, which causes cracks and a weaker top layer. Keep it moist and covered rather than letting heat speed things up.

Can it rain on new concrete?

Rain in the first few hours can wash out or pit the surface, so cover fresh concrete if rain is forecast. After it has set hard, rain is actually good for curing.

How long should I water new concrete?

Keep it moist for at least the first seven days. Covering it with plastic or using a curing compound means you don't have to keep spraying it.

Sources

  • American Concrete Institute (ACI) curing and cold-weather concreting guidance — seven-day minimum curing for ordinary concrete and protection from freezing until about 500 psi.
  • Strength milestones are typical values for ordinary portland cement concrete at about 70°F; actual times depend on the mix, temperature and product. Follow bag instructions or your supplier's recommendations.
